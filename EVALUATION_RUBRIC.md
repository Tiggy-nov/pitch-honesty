# Evaluation Rubric

The scorecard is diagnostic, not mathematical. Do not use an average to hide a fatal weakness.

Score each category from 1–10.

| Category | 1–3 | 4–6 | 7–8 | 9–10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Problem severity | Nice-to-have | Real but deferrable | Painful + budgeted | Mission-critical / structural |
| Why now | Generic trend | Some enabling change | Clear structural shift | Shift makes company newly inevitable |
| Product delta | Feature | Incremental improvement | 10x workflow improvement | New capability / architecture |
| Founder quality | Weak fit | Credible | Exceptional fit/execution | Rare insight + magnetism |
| Technical differentiation | Commodity | Some novelty | Difficult system advantage | Deep compounding edge |
| Customer evidence | Interest only | Pilots | Production / references | Pull, expansion, repeatability |
| Repeatability | Services-heavy | Partial standardisation | Repeatable sales/deployment | Scales predictably |
| Business model | Unclear | Viable | Attractive | Structurally exceptional |
| Market scale | Small | Moderate | Venture-scale | Category/platform-scale |
| Expansion | Hand-wavy | Adjacent modules | Logical land-and-expand | Strategic control point |
| Defensibility | None | Temporary lead | Multiple compounding assets | Strong structural moat |
| Capital efficiency | Poor | Normal | Efficient | Exceptional leverage |
| Fundraising readiness | Hard story | Some interest | Credible next-round path | Obvious high-quality path |
| Narrative clarity | Confusing | Understandable | Crisp | Instantly repeatable |
| Memorability | Generic | Some hooks | Distinctive | Hard to forget |

## Gating questions

Before calling a deal or deck ready, identify the three variables that actually control the decision.

Typical fatal gates:
- no urgent customer pain,
- no repeatable wedge,
- high services dependence with weak software leverage,
- no credible path to venture-scale revenue,
- no differentiated product or structural advantage,
- weak founder-market fit,
- evidence contradicts the narrative,
- next financing milestone is implausible.

## Final classifications

### GREEN
The underlying investment story is strong enough to build. Remaining issues are mainly evidence depth, sequencing, or communication.

### AMBER
The company is interesting, but one or more major investor beliefs are unsupported. Narrative work can proceed only if missing evidence is explicit.

### RED
The core thesis is weak or incoherent. Do not use storytelling to manufacture conviction.

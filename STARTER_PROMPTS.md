# Starter Prompts

## Pass 1 — Investment Truth
> Run **Pass 1 — Investment Truth** on this company. Ignore slide design and persuasive wording. Tell me whether the underlying venture case is actually strong, what is proven, what is assumed, and which issues are storytelling problems versus business problems. Give me the GREEN / AMBER / RED gate.

## Pass 2 — Narrative Architecture
> Using the Pass 1 conclusions, run **Pass 2 — Narrative Architecture**. Build the investor belief ladder, one-page strategic narrative, investor memory sentence, tension curve, and ideal slide sequence. For every slide give me the investor question, conclusion headline, evidence, visual idea, and narrative transition.

## Pass 3 — Investor Room
> Assume the founder has just presented this deck and left the room. Run **Pass 3 — Investor Room Simulation**. Be realistic rather than supportive. Simulate the skeptical seed investor, Series A investor, IC sponsor, hostile partner, closed-door debate, memory test, retelling test, competition test, Monday IC test, and final investment decision.

## Full three-pass review
> Review this company and deck using the complete **Investor Narrative Architect** workflow. Run Pass 1, then Pass 2, then Pass 3. Finish with the rebuild loop and a final Founder Narrative Pack. Do not redesign weak slides until you have decided whether the problem is narrative or structural.

## Rebuild an existing deck
> Preserve all important factual information in the existing deck, but rebuild the narrative from first principles. First identify the intended investor beliefs, then determine which current slides support them, which should move, which should be deleted, and what is missing. Give me a slide-by-slide replacement architecture before rewriting copy.

## Founder coaching mode
> I am coaching the founder before fundraising. Use the skill to identify the five things the founder must be able to explain simply, the five questions that expose weak thinking, the strongest 30-second company narrative, and the ten hardest investor questions they should rehearse.

## Board mode
> I sit on the board of this company. Use the Investor Narrative Architect to separate company-strategy issues from fundraising-story issues. Tell me what I should push management to fix in the business versus what can be solved through communication.

## Monday IC compression
> Pretend you are the investment partner presenting this deal at Monday IC in seven minutes. Compress the story into the fewest beliefs required for the partnership to understand why the company matters, why now, why it can win, and why it can return the fund. Then tell me which objection is most likely to kill the deal.

## Headline pass
> Read the deck only through its slide headlines. Rewrite them so that the headlines alone tell the complete investment story. Use conclusions, not topic labels. Do not introduce facts not present in the source material.

## Story vs truth diagnostic
> For every weakness you find, tag it as one of: STORYTELLING, EVIDENCE, PRODUCT, BUSINESS MODEL, STRATEGY, MARKET, TEAM. Do not recommend a narrative fix for a non-narrative problem.

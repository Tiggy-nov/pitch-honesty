# Research Foundations

This skill synthesises multiple approaches. The sources below are included for provenance and further study; the skill is not a verbatim reproduction of any source.

## Reece Chowdhry — phase-aware fundraising storytelling

**What VCs really mean when they say "be good at storytelling" — Part 1 of 3**  
LinkedIn, 1 October 2026  
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/what-vcs-really-mean-when-say-good-storytelling-part-reece-chowdhry-kohve/

Key principles incorporated:
- fundraising storytelling changes by phase;
- the first call is an attention filter;
- the opening has a very small guaranteed attention budget;
- simplicity is evidence of command;
- the listener should be able to repeat what the company does;
- a founder introduction should be compressed to a few high-signal chapters;
- the first phase is about earning the right to continue, not explaining everything.

Only Part 1 was publicly available when v1.1.0 was prepared. The skill does not attribute unpublished Part 2/3 specifics to Reece.

**A Q&A with Concept Ventures Founding Partner Reece Chowdhry**  
Early Early Stage, 9 June 2026  
https://earlyearlystage.substack.com/p/a-q-and-a-with-concept-ventures-founding

Relevant principle: at pre-seed, founders can over-index on decks and plans; investor belief is heavily influenced by founder conviction, lived understanding of the problem, and confidence in the team's ability to figure things out.

## Y Combinator — clarity and repeatability

**Michael Seibel — How to Pitch Your Company**  
https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/how-to-pitch-your-company

Key principles incorporated: start with what the company does; make the explanation extremely simple; the first explanation should create interest rather than exhaustive understanding; user-path descriptions can outperform abstraction; test whether another person can explain the company back accurately; unique insight should teach the investor something specific.

**Kevin Hale — How to Pitch Your Startup**  
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=17XZGUX_9iM

Key principles incorporated: startup idea as a hypothesis for rapid growth; problem, solution, and insight; clarity, concision, legibility, and reproducibility; remove ambiguity, complexity, and jargon.

## Nancy Duarte — tension and contrast

**The Secret Structure of Great Talks**  
https://www.ted.com/talks/nancy_duarte_the_secret_structure_of_great_talks

**Duarte — Storytelling Presentation Skills**  
https://www.duarte.com/blog/storytelling-presentation-skills/

Key principle incorporated: alternate between "what is" and "what could be" to create narrative tension rather than presenting a flat report.

## David S. Rose — founder signal and presentation medium

**How to Pitch to a VC**  
https://www.ted.com/talks/david_s_rose_how_to_pitch_to_a_vc

Key principles incorporated: investors are evaluating the entrepreneur as well as the business; the pitch should demonstrate integrity, knowledge, leadership, commitment, vision, realism, and coachability; a live presentation and a standalone handout are different communication objects.

## Michael Moritz / Vertex Ventures US — mission and the mountain

**Storytelling should be part of the business school curriculum, says legendary investor Sir Michael Moritz**  
https://medium.com/vvus/storytelling-should-be-part-of-the-business-school-curriculum-says-legendary-investor-sir-michael-ffb57d8cec39

Key principles incorporated: storytelling is a core founder and leadership skill; mission should be short, sharp, and ambitious; frame the mission as a mountain to climb; connect the summit to credible intermediate milestones or "base camps."

## Andy Raskin — strategic narrative

**Strategic Story Training**  
https://andyraskin.com/sales-story-training/

Key principle incorporated: storytelling is not decorative anecdote; it is a strategic structure that gets an audience excited about a future the company is committed to helping create.

## Research discipline

The framework deliberately distinguishes concepts directly supported by these sources, synthesis across sources, and additional investor-analysis methods created for this skill. No source should be treated as a substitute for company-specific evidence.
